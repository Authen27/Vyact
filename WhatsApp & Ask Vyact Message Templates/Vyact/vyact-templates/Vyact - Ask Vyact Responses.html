In-app assistant
Same seven turns as WhatsApp. More room, and no PIN.
The user is authenticated here, so every figure answers straight away — net worth included. Copy is ours to change freely, which is also where Hinglish mirroring lives.
Response shape, by bucket
A · Capture
Echo what was recorded, then the undo. If a field was inferred by a model, ask before saving. Never more than one question.
B · Interpret
Headline figure first, then the context that makes it mean something, then the two or three likely follow-ups as chips.
C · Forecast
Verdict, the number behind it, then the assumption it rests on — always. Never a flat “no”; name the date the answer changes.
Hard limits
  • Never a figure the app didn't calculate — no “roughly a third”.
  • Never investment, tax or legal advice.
  • Say “I don't have enough yet” rather than estimate thinly.
  • Every inbound message gets a reply.

R3 · Forecast flow. Every forecast answer carries a violet-ruled assumption line, on both surfaces. “How much can I spend today” is the most useful forecast in the product and the easiest to get wrong — so it names both assumptions rather than presenting a daily allowance as fact.

Response anatomy
Four parts, in this order

1 · The answer, first word

The figure or the verdict leads. Never “Let me check that for you” — an Acknowledge turn is only permitted when the next turn genuinely takes a moment.

2 · The context that gives it meaning

A percentage of budget, a comparison to the user's own last month, days remaining. One line. This is what separates an answer from a lookup.

3 · The assumption — Forecast only

Violet rule, plain sentence, in the same message. A wrong bill reminder is an annoyance; a wrong “you can afford this” costs money.

4 · Two or three likely follow-ups

Chips are the next question, not navigation. “Why is it up?” beats “Open reports”. Max three, and never a chip that just repeats the answer.

Never

  • A figure no calculation produced.
  • An apology, or an exclamation mark.
  • Congratulating someone for logging a spend.
  • A flat “no” to an affordability question.
  • Investment, tax or legal advice.

Response anatomy. Applied to all 24 responses on this board. The ordering matters more than the wording: leading with the figure is what makes a two-line reply feel competent, and putting the assumption after the verdict keeps a forecast honest without making it timid.

Interpret responses. Every comparison is the user against their own past — never against an average, a cohort or a “people like you”, which is where finance apps start moralising. “How am I doing?” is the hardest of the nine: the answer has to be specific enough to be useful and calm enough not to alarm, so it leads with one word, then four facts, then the single thing worth watching.

Forecast responses and boundaries. The four refusals are the most important copy on this board. Each one gives something real instead of a wall: the card-versus-invest question withholds the advice but hands over the interest cost that actually decides it; “how much next month” admits thin data rather than extrapolating one week; and the delete request names the exact path plus an export first. A refusal that routes is the difference between a careful assistant and a useless one.
